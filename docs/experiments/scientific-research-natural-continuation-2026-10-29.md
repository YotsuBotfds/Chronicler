## Question and fixed cohort

After a common history of naturally admitted institutions and finite research,
what additional paid work, received information and paid adoption occurs when
new research continues, compared with pausing new trial admission while keeping
the existing archive and already-paid work? This is a development exposure and
mechanism study. It registers no efficacy threshold, significance test, default
promotion or speedup claim.

Use exactly **seeds 2000 through 2007**, in that order, ordinary **template
worlds with 8 regions and 4 civilizations**, hybrid agents, simulate-only,
**180 completed turns**. Do not inspect outcomes to select seeds, replace a
seed, extend the window or add an arm. These seed IDs are a prospective fixed
cohort; no claim of statistical independence between seeds or within a world
follows from their different IDs.

Use normal generation and action selection: no supplied world, seeded academy,
forced DEVELOP choice, treasury/ecology/practice override, or favorable
eligibility filter. Explicitly enable scholarship, techniques, scientific claims
and finite research agendas; retain the source's other ordinary defaults.
Keep the other new expansion switches at their ordinary disabled setting.
Natural here describes generation, institutional admission and endogenous work
under these declared experimental switches, not an unflagged default run.

The shared science controls are complete and fixed: `withhold_information:
false`, `transmission_enabled: true`, `uninformative_tests: false`,
`publication_rule: funded-context-v2`. Apply them through the existing declared
parent experiment seam before its first tick. Preserve all original thresholds,
costs, funding windows, reservation rules, climate and measurement functions.

Then report each fork's source-dated flow in separate stages:

1. New agendas and actual funded originals/replications at sources 60–179,
   with actual payer, cost, context, canonical topic ID and cycle membership.
   Renewing a topic is not a new independent question or measurement block.
2. Terminal work, separating trials funded before 60 from new admissions;
   completed, cancelled, failed, withheld, uninformative and still-running rows
   remain distinct. Expense includes every paid status, not only publication.
3. Actually received information by recipient/trial/date, separating old work
   from new work. Publication, transmission, receipt and applicability are
   different stages; archive counts do not prove current use or independence.
4. Actual paid adoption rows: dates, claim IDs, shares, confidence used, costs
   and availability of relevant received evidence. Phase-10 receipt at source
   S cannot explain that same source's earlier Phase-4 allocation. Use the
   confidence stored on the paid row; do not substitute the end-turn belief.
   Evidence received by S−1 is a conservative prior-availability observation;
   same-source receipt timing alone does not certify pre-payment availability.

Disclose calendar/context and the two-of-four-season limitation of the
persisted six-turn payment cadence. Absent paid use in an unsampled context
is missing use exposure, not a negative efficacy estimate. A reused topic can
have both old and new receipts: label those histories rather than attributing
its entire allocation to the newest trial. Finite capacity, eligibility,
survival, practice and treasury feedback may cause unequal realized spending.

Give seed-level active-minus-pause differences for the stage counts and costs,
with all-eight-seed totals and each paired row. Descriptive world outcomes

plot grain is not inventory output, and a confidence/receipt change is not an
established welfare benefit. No within-world record bootstrap or post-outcome
exposed-only estimate is a confirmatory result.
