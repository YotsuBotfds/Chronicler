## 09 War, military, nomads, maritime, exploration

**Map**
- Campaigns (M60) are the production war model, default on for new CLI/live worlds (`war/params.py:CAMPAIGNS_DEFAULT_FOR_NEW_WORLDS`, `main.py:1615`, `live.py:1311`). `WorldState.campaigns_enabled` defaults False (`models.py:1165`), so legacy and directly built worlds keep instant resolution.
- Turn loop: `simulation.py:2231-2233` calls `war.runtime.tick_campaigns` after nomads and before Phase 6. `simulation.py:2564-2566` calls `end_of_turn_sync` at Phase 10.
- WAR action hook: `action_engine.py:419-421` routes to `war.runtime.declare_or_press_war` when campaigns are on. Otherwise `action_engine.py:436` calls legacy `resolve_war` (`action_engine.py:747-~905`), which resolves the whole war in one call.
- `war/` package, about 2,900 lines: `runtime` (orchestration, hook), `armies` (raise, casualties, desertion, generals, roster reconcile), `battles` (power, fight, sieges, skirmish), `logistics` (per-army tick and movement), `occupation`, `peace` (aims, war score, treaties), `ai` (orders), `common` (context, stat routing), `params`.
- Adjacent: `war_requisition.py` (517), `war_consequences.py` (312), `campaign_logistics.py` (762, detached kernel), `campaign_snapshot_adapter.py` (631), `campaign_order_preview.py` (169), `nomads.py` (1520), `maritime.py` (1658), `exploration.py` (326). Only the war package and the legacy path were read in depth; the rest is unverified.
- Campaign turn order (`runtime.py:56-81`): reconcile, validate, rebind, mobilize, orders, logistics, drop-empty, move, field battles, sieges/liberation, occupations, peace, demobilize, flush losses.

**Findings**
1. [med] bug — Campaign early returns can drop events. `runtime.py:84-148` calls `reconcile_wars(ctx)` first. The "could not muster" return (`runtime.py:~108`) and the truce return skip `world.events_timeline.extend(ctx.events)` and `flush_military_losses`. Any event that `reconcile_wars` emitted is lost (inferred from code path; not run). Suggested action: extend events on every return path.
2. [med] fragile — Non-roster casualties ignore the real drain. `armies.py:330-365` discards the return of `drain_population` (which can remove fewer people than asked) but still subtracts `count` from `army.soldiers`. Soldiers can vanish with no population loss. The drain also spreads across every region the civ controls, not the army's region. Suggested action: subtract the actual drained amount, or assert it equals the request.
3. [med] fragile — Roster casualties drain by a stale region. `armies.py:348-350` uses the roster's stored region index, which is refreshed only in `reconcile_rosters` (`armies.py:~440`). Suggested action: drain the region the agent occupies at the time, or document the lag.
4. [med] sim-depth/fragile — Mercenaries are not real soldiers. `battles.py:99-125` and `battles.py:166-190` derive mercenary head count from company strength and reduce strength on loss. They have no roster, no morale and no logistics. Their quality is hardcoded at morale 650 (mercs) and 600 (garrison) in `battles.py:137-150`.
5. [med] fragile — Assault victims can be dead when the occupation starts. `battles.py:226-234` destroys any side under `MIN_ARMY`, winner included. `runtime.py` (siege branch, about line 272) then calls `start_occupation(..., armies[0])` with the pre-fight list. The skirmish branch can pass `None` as the army (`survivors[0] if survivors else None`). Whether `occupation.start_occupation` handles either case is unverified.
6. [med] dead-code — `held_regions` has no caller. `war/common.py:153-154`; grep for `held_regions(` finds only a local variable in `peace.py:247`. Delete.
7. [med] duplication — Recruit gating is written twice. `armies.py:54-64` (`can_raise`) repeats the cap, cooldown and affordability checks in `raise_army` (`armies.py:168-190`). Extract one predicate.
8. [med] duplication/complexity — Two war models coexist. Legacy `resolve_war` (`action_engine.py:747-~905`, about 150 lines) stays live for `--no-campaigns` and about 12 tests (`tests/test_action_engine.py`, `test_simulation.py`, `test_diplomacy.py`, `test_severity.py` and others). It has its own treasury, military and stability paths, so tuning changes must be made twice.
9. [low] fragile — Determinism and state hygiene. `armies.py` `rebind_rosters` keys its marker on `id(sim)`; an id can be reused after garbage collection. `general_of` (`armies.py:~250-260`) mutates `army.general` on read. Battle RNG is keyed by (war, region, kind, attacker) only (`battles.py:~152`), so two same-kind engagements in one turn share one stream (inferred).
10. [low] fragile — Magic numbers. `logistics.py` `run_logistics` tests `get_season_id(turn) == 3` for winter, with no named constant.
11. [low] fragile — `common.py` `add_stat` direct path truncates with `int()` (`common.py:189-200`), while `flush_military_losses` rounds. `projected_treasury` (`common.py:203-213`) reads private accumulator fields `_changes` and `_keep_applied_up_to`.
12. [low] coupling — `end_of_turn_sync` (`runtime.py:~329-355`) reads `world.livelihoods` and `world._agent_bridge` directly.
13. [med] dead-for-turn-loop — `campaign_order_preview.py` and `campaign_snapshot_adapter.py` are not in the turn loop. Their only callers are scripts, detached sessions (`snapshot_campaign_sessions.py`, `experiment_sessions.py`) and tests. Both are pinned in `scripts/testing/native_shards_manifest.json` and in `tests/fixtures/reference_baselines/`.

**Simulation depth**
- Supply is "held regions" only (`logistics.py:38`). Foraging draws on `region.population // 5` and never touches ecology or food stock (`logistics.py:~80`). Wiring forage to the region food surplus would make campaigns ecologically coupled.
- Sieges have no supply cut-off for the garrison. Only a fixed siege disease term and civilian drain exist (`battles.py:366-414`, `params.py`).
- Terrain is limited to move cost, attrition and defense (`params.py`). There are no roads, rivers as movement, or seasonal movement penalties beyond winter attrition.
- Military technology feeds `quality` through an era step (`battles.py:53-63`), plus the optional techniques factor. Unit types and composition do not exist.
- Mercenaries could become real companies with a roster, wages and desertion (see finding 4).

**Confidence** — Verified by reading code and grep: campaign/legacy wiring, `runtime.py`, `armies.py`, `battles.py`, `logistics.py`, the `common.py` helpers, the callers of the preview and adapter modules, and the `held_regions` dead-code check. Partly read: `peace.py` to line 200 only, `occupation.py`, `ai.py`. Not read: `nomads.py`, `maritime.py`, `exploration.py`, `war_requisition.py`, `war_consequences.py`, `campaign_logistics.py`. Findings 1, 3 and 9 are inferred from code paths and not run. No tests or simulations were run.
